# Trainer J hero photo brief

## The photo to send

Please provide one horizontal and one vertical photo from the same coaching setup, with Jacob actively coaching a real client, both people placed to the right, and clean darker space on the left for the headline and buttons.

This should feel like a real coaching moment, not a trainer portrait. Jacob can be cueing a controlled squat, hinge, or cable movement with one relatable adult client. The expression should feel focused, calm, patient, and safe. No shouting, crossed arms, staged celebration, medical imagery, or exaggerated fitness pose.

## Composition

- Desktop: 16:9, at least 2400 by 1350 pixels.
- Mobile: 4:5 or 3:4, at least 1080 by 1350 pixels.
- Keep Jacob and the client within the right 35 to 40 percent of the horizontal frame.
- Leave the left 45 to 50 percent visually quiet for copy.
- On mobile, keep both faces in the upper-right area so the lower-left remains usable.
- Do not place important details near the outside 8 percent of any edge.

## Light, color, and setting

- Use a clean Irvine training space with enough equipment to feel credible, but no clutter, bystanders, or competing logos.
- Use soft directional light at roughly 45 degrees with natural skin tones.
- Let the background fall about one stop darker than the people.
- Put Jacob in navy or charcoal. Put the client in muted blue, gray, or an earth tone.
- Avoid bright red, neon clothing, and blown-out windows.

## Why this direction

The headline is about building strength a person can trust. A real coaching interaction proves attention and safety before the visitor reads a word. It also speaks directly to people who feel intimidated, have lost momentum, or are returning after time away.

## Delivery and rights

- Send the original full-resolution files, not social media downloads or screenshots.
- JPEG, PNG, TIFF, or high-quality HEIC is fine.
- Keep both the horizontal and vertical originals from the same setup.
- Confirm that Jacob, the client, the photographer, and the facility have approved website and marketing use.

The current hero uses the supplied 600 by 900 Adobe Express cutout as a transparent foreground subject over a designed navy background. The subject and background layers are separate, so a future horizontal and vertical coaching photo can replace the designed background without rebuilding the copy or page structure.
